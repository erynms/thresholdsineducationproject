Key Theme: From Detection to Critical Engagement

Shifting the Pedagogical Question

One of the most significant shifts represented across this special issue is a fundamental reframing of how educators approach generative AI in the classroom.

The Shift

OLD Question

"How do we catch students using AI?"

This question positions AI as a threat, students as potential cheaters, and detection tools as the primary response. It frames the relationship between educators and students as adversarial, prioritizing surveillance over pedagogy.

NEW Question

"How do we teach students to engage AI critically?"

This reframing positions AI as an object of study rather than simply a tool (or threat). It assumes students can and will make thoughtful choices when given appropriate frameworks and opportunities for exploration.

GenAI as Object of Study

Several articles in this issue demonstrate how treating generative AI as something to be studied—rather than simply used or banned—opens new pedagogical possibilities:

  • Students analyze AI-generated text to understand its rhetorical patterns, limitations, and biases
  • Collaborative annotation becomes a method for building critical awareness of how AI constructs language
  • Comparative analysis between student writing and AI-generated text reveals what makes human writing distinctive
  • Experimentation with prompts teaches students about the relationship between input and output in AI systems

Building Metalinguistic Awareness

When students engage critically with AI-generated writing, they develop metalinguistic awareness—a deeper understanding of how language works, how texts are constructed, and what choices writers make (consciously or unconsciously).

This awareness is valuable regardless of whether students ultimately choose to use AI tools in their own writing. The process of critique and analysis builds exactly the kind of critical thinking skills that writing instruction aims to develop.

Pedagogical Methods

The articles in this issue demonstrate several concrete methods for moving from detection to critical engagement:

  • Annotation assignments: Students collectively annotate and critique AI-generated writing
  • Rhetorical analysis: Examining the choices (and limitations) visible in AI text
  • Revision exercises: Students improve AI-generated drafts, identifying weaknesses and gaps
  • Comparative writing: Students write both with and without AI to understand the differences
  • Reflection assignments: Students articulate their choices about when and how to use AI

Implications

This shift from detection to critical engagement represents more than a tactical change—it reflects a fundamental philosophical commitment to treating students as capable of ethical decision-making and critical thinking. It moves the conversation from policing to pedagogy, from surveillance to education.
