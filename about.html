About the Issue

Journal Context

Thresholds is an academic journal dedicated to educational research and practice. This special issue brings together eight articles examining generative AI in writing and composition classrooms, representing diverse perspectives from composition, rhetoric, writing studies, and educational technology.

Guest Editors: Marc Watkins & Stephen Monroe (University of Mississippi)

The issue represents a critical intervention in ongoing conversations about AI in education—moving beyond polarized debates to offer pragmatic, evidence-based approaches grounded in actual classroom experiences.

The Moment: Why This Issue Now?

The ChatGPT Disruption

In November 2022, OpenAI's public launch of ChatGPT fundamentally disrupted higher education. Seemingly overnight, faculty found themselves facing what the editors describe as "a giant public experiment no one asked for."

Key characteristics of this moment:

  • Widespread student use of generative AI tools without institutional guidance or support
  • Competing narratives: complete resistance versus enthusiastic adoption
  • Faculty left to navigate pedagogical challenges without adequate training or resources
  • Urgent need for evidence-based approaches rather than speculation

This issue's intervention: Rather than choosing sides in the resistance-versus-adoption debate, this collection stakes out a pragmatic, exploratory middle ground. The articles acknowledge both the remarkable capabilities of generative AI and its significant limitations.

Editors' Framing: Navigating the Crossroads

Beyond Hype and Panic

The guest editors position themselves—and invite readers to join them—as critical investigators rather than AI advocates or AI refusers. This stance is characterized by several key principles:

Core Principles

  • Transparency and ethical usage: Open disclosure about AI use by both faculty and students
  • Exploratory experimentation: Low-stakes opportunities for students to engage with AI tools
  • Evidence-based approaches: Classroom research and empirical findings over speculation
  • Critical examination: Acknowledging both capabilities and limitations

This framing positions higher education not as a victim of technological disruption, but as uniquely positioned to study generative AI critically and guide students toward thoughtful engagement.

Scope of the Issue

What These Eight Articles Examine

The articles in this special issue cover a wide range of approaches to generative AI in writing classrooms:

  • Collaborative annotation of AI-generated writing as a pedagogical method
  • Student agency and voice when using generative AI tools
  • Authorship and collaboration redefined in the age of AI
  • Practical frameworks for "collaborative intelligence" between humans and AI
  • Generative AI as an assessment and feedback tool
  • Rhetorical choices and student decision-making processes
  • Rethinking learning and assessment paradigms for the AI era
  • Transparency in AI systems and their limitations

Together, these articles demonstrate that students can and do make ethical, thoughtful choices about AI use when provided with appropriate frameworks, guidance, and opportunities for critical engagement.

Moving Forward

This special issue represents a crucial moment in the ongoing conversation about AI in education. Rather than offering definitive answers, it provides frameworks, evidence, and approaches that educators can adapt to their own contexts. The work here acknowledges the complexity of the moment while maintaining optimism about higher education's capacity to navigate it thoughtfully.
