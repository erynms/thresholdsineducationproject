Collaborative Intelligence: Towards Practical, Critical and Cooperative Teaching & Learning with AI

D.B. Nelson, A.E. Gackiere, S.E. LeGrand, & D.A. Guberman

Nelson, D.B., Gackiere, A.E., LeGrand, S.E., & Guberman, D.A. (2025). Collaborative intelligence: Towards practical, critical and cooperative teaching & learning with AI. Thresholds in Education, 48(1), 57-80.

Summary

Looking both forwards and backwards in time, Nelson and fellow authors propose a framework for dealing with the AI tools that we have and the ones we don't know about yet. They argue that involving students as we adjust learning expectations and classroom management, adjusting to a shifting power structure in the teacher-student relationship. If we do not embrace these shifts, Nelson and his team believe that we run the risk of being replaced as teachers, not by administration, but by the students themselves.

Nelson et al. embrace the paradigm that generative AI is disrupting and will continue to disrupt the status quo in academia. They introduce four practices for instructors to use, working towards what they refer to as algorithmic literacy. Further, they propose a four-part process for mutual engagement with generative AI tools, steps that teachers can move through, involving their students in their own growth.

The authors' goals are to encourage other teachers to come out of hiding and work with their students to build digital literacy and to remake the educational experience together.

Key Contributions

  • Framework for collaborative intelligence between humans and AI
  • Four practices for developing algorithmic literacy
  • Four-part process for mutual engagement with AI tools
  • Emphasis on shifting power dynamics in the classroom
  • Call for educators to emerge from resistance into collaboration
