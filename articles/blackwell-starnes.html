"I Prefer My Own Writing": Engaging First-Year Writers' Agency with Generative AI

K. Blackwell-Starnes

Blackwell-Starnes, K. (2025). "I prefer my own writing": Engaging first-year writers' agency with generative AI. Thresholds in Education, 48(1), 25-39.

Summary

Blackwell-Starnes investigates how embedding generative AI (specifically ChatGPT) into a first-semester college writing course in Fall 2023 can promote student agency. The study examines how students used ChatGPT during invention and revision phases of major writing assignments, showing that students became more thoughtful about accepting or rejecting feedback and more reflective on their writing decisions.

The author argues that when treated as a tool within a structured pedagogy of conferencing and discussion about writing, generative AI can empower writer agency rather than undermine it. The paper demonstrates that by combining AI use with low-stakes conferences and reflective practices, students demonstrate increased ownership of their writing process.

The argument suggests that incorporating AI ethically and transparently with student agency at the center can shift how writing instruction happens in first-year composition. This study is directly relevant to AI rhetorics because it explores how generative AI technologies influence writing practices, student-writer agency, and the rhetorical relationship between human author and machine aid. It raises questions about how AI tools mediate authorship, revision, and decision-making in writing contexts.

Key Contributions

  • Evidence that structured AI use promotes rather than undermines agency
  • Students' preference for their own writing over AI-generated text
  • Framework combining AI tools with conferencing and reflection
  • Demonstrates increased student ownership of writing process
  • Ethical integration of AI with student agency centered
