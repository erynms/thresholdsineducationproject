Black Boxes Revisited: Understanding GenAI Responses to Student Writing Across the Curriculum

M. Velez, Z. Reed, D. Chamberlain, & C. Aydiner

Velez, M., Reed, Z., Chamberlain, D., & Aydiner, C. (2025). Black boxes revisited: Understanding GenAI responses to student writing across the curriculum. Thresholds in Education, 48(1), 57-98.

Summary

Velez et al. investigate how ChatGPT-3.5 evaluates student feedback, revealing problems with the LLM inventing criteria, giving inaccurate feedback, and using inconsistent scoring across student submissions. Using grounded theory methods, they identify six key themes throughout the feedback.

They conclude that AI's abilities (at that time) to conceptualize the rhetorical situation and take it into account when grading leave the LLM in no better condition to evaluate and grade than many of the non-AI tools available at the time, reproducing the same concerns and criticisms leveled at tools such as EssayGrader.

The authors suggest that more transparency in the evaluative process is needed before digital grading can replace human assessment.

Key Contributions

  • Empirical investigation of ChatGPT-3.5's feedback capabilities
  • Identification of six key themes in AI-generated feedback
  • Documentation of AI's limitations in understanding rhetorical situations
  • Critique of AI grading tools as reproducing existing problems
  • Call for transparency in automated evaluation processes

Implications

This article serves as an important cautionary note about the rush to adopt AI for assessment purposes. It demonstrates that generative AI, at least in its current form, may not offer the improvements over existing automated grading tools that enthusiasts claim—and may in fact reproduce their most problematic features.
