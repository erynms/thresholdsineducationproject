Rhetorical Choices & Voice: Generative AI in the First-Year Composition Classroom

G. Krueger

Krueger, G. (2025). Rhetorical Choices & Voice: Generative AI in the First-Year Composition Classroom. Thresholds in Education, 48(1), 99-110.

Summary

Krueger argues that when students use GenAI for generating a rough draft of an essay and then follow the normal revision steps from there, the same rhetorical and critical thinking skills are developed as when students complete the drafting from scratch.

His article walks teachers through an assignment he uses in his first year writing course, demonstrating that students develop a new kind of co-agency without sacrificing the skills development writing teachers value.

For students who struggle with introducing their students to ethical use for these tools, this article could be revolutionary, and for those opposed to incorporating GenAI tools into their pedagogical practices, this article will challenge their paradigm.

Key Contributions

  • Practical assignment design for ethical AI use in first-year composition
  • Evidence that revision of AI-generated drafts develops rhetorical skills
  • Framework for understanding co-agency between students and AI
  • Challenge to assumptions that AI use undermines skill development
  • Demonstration that AI can be integrated without sacrificing pedagogical values

Implications

This article offers a practical middle path for educators uncertain about how to approach AI in writing classrooms. It demonstrates that the revision process—a cornerstone of writing pedagogy—can be just as valuable when applied to AI-generated text as to student-generated text, as long as students engage critically and rhetorically with the material.
