Can AI Be a Co-Author?: How Generative AI Challenges the Boundaries of Authorship in a General Education Writing Class

H. Ryan, D. Abramov, S. Acker, & S. Elkins

Ryan, H., Abramov, D., Acker, S., & Elkins, S. (2025). Can AI be a co-author?: How generative AI challenges the boundaries of authorship in a general education writing class. Thresholds in Education, 48(1), 40-56.

Summary

Ryan et al. examine how students in a general-education writing class engaged with generative AI tools in the context of co-authoring texts, and the resulting implications for what counts as authorship. The authors argue that generative AI complicates traditional notions of writer identity, ownership, and the division between human and machine contributions.

They present evidence from classroom practice showing how students navigated collaboration with AI—choosing when to accept, modify, or reject AI-generated suggestions and how that affected their understanding of writing as a rhetorical act. Students' decision-making processes revealed emerging literacy practices around negotiating agency with algorithmic writing partners.

The paper concludes that to sustain meaningful authorship in the era of AI, writing instruction must explicitly address the boundaries, affordances, and ethics of AI co-writing. This study is directly relevant to AI rhetorics because it interrogates how generative AI reshapes writing practices, authorship ethos, and the rhetorical positioning of human versus machine in knowledge production. It highlights how big-data-trained tools mediate not just output, but the very process and identity of writing.

Key Contributions

  • Examination of AI as collaborative writing partner
  • Complicates traditional notions of authorship and ownership
  • Evidence of student decision-making with AI suggestions
  • Framework for ethical AI co-writing in writing instruction
  • Critical perspective on human-machine rhetorical positioning
